How this works (and its limits)

Android + Chrome: reads the real ambient light sensor (lux). iPhone: iOS doesn't let websites read the light sensor, so the camera's view of the sky is used as a brightness proxy instead.

Phone sensors only see visible light, never UV. SunD estimates UV by comparing your measured brightness with the theoretical clear-sky brightness for the sun's exact position (computed from your time & location — no internet needed). Clouds and haze dim the light, and the estimate drops with it.

The golden rule: this only works outdoors (or at an open window) with the sensor/camera facing the sky. Glass blocks the UV-B that makes vitamin D but lets visible light through — indoors behind glass, real UV is ~zero no matter how bright it looks. Expect ±2–3 UV index of error: a guide, not a measurement.

Vitamin D model: 1,000 IU ≈ exposing ~25% of skin to ¼ of your minimal erythemal dose (MED). MED scales with Fitzpatrick skin type. The burn bar shows progress toward one full MED — stop well before it fills.

⚠️ Rough estimate only — not medical advice. Never use this to justify longer sun exposure; when in doubt, cover up. Runs 100% on your device. No data leaves the phone.
